# Facilitator mode: running the method with a team

Use this when someone is leading the method for a group: a department, a cohort of participants, or a leadership team. No single training session drives adoption on its own. The gap that matters is between a team finishing a course and that same team shipping helpers that deliver measurable results. The format below is built to close that gap.

Every engagement follows the same three-part arc: **discovery, workshop, sustainment.**

## 1. Discovery (one to three weeks before)

**Purpose:** learn where the team really is, so the material is framed in their language and their examples.

- **Sponsor conversation first.** Agree on the "why," the "what's in it for me" for participants, what the sponsor will do (open the first session, attend the final pitches), and what decisions the sponsor is prepared to make at the end.
- **Short survey** to all participants (ten minutes):
  1. What is your role, and what does a typical week look like?
  2. Which tasks take the most time relative to their value?
  3. Where do errors or rework happen most?
  4. Which AI tools do you use today, how often, and for what?
  5. How confident are you using AI for real work? (1 to 5)
  6. What worries you about AI in your work?
- **Subject-matter expert pre-work.** Ask two or three experts to map one workflow each using the Step 1 step table, and collect five to ten real examples per workflow.
- **Customize.** Rewrite the session examples using what discovery found: "here is how this shows up in your work."

## 2. The workshop

Run it as six sessions (one per step) over one to three weeks, or compress it into one or two full days. Participants work on **their own** workflows the entire time, in small teams of two to four.

| Session | Focus | Hands-on activity | Artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Find | Map the work, funnel the ideas | Each team maps one workflow from its last real instance; whole group sorts pain points (sticky notes on a wall in person, a shared board remotely) and scores them | Step table, scored candidates, baseline |
| 2. Shape | Redesign first, then find the AI fit | Circle and underline steps; walk the ladder of fixes; run a taste test on three real examples | Future-state map, AI fit verdict |
| 3. Source | Build, buy, or combine | Teams argue the case for each option, then decide; complete the data map and risk tier | Sourcing decision, data map |
| 4. Fund | Put numbers on it | Fill the ROI inputs, run the calculator, set thresholds | Three-scenario case, success criteria |
| 5. Build and Test | Make it real, then try to break it | Build v0.1 in the approved tool; run the test set; swap helpers between teams for red teaming | Working helper, test results, failure log |
| 6. Prove | Make it stick and make the case | Build the scoreboard and launch plan; rehearse the pitch | Impact plan, pitch |

**Formats that keep people active:**
- Breakout and discussion rounds every session, so teams can push back on each other's ideas.
- In person, kinetic sorting: participants physically move pain points from "everything" to "buildable use cases," and stand on the build, buy, or combine side of the room to argue it out.
- Live building, with the waiting left in. Seeing a helper fail and get fixed teaches more than a polished demo.

**The capstone.** Teams pitch their use cases and business cases to the sponsor and decision makers, the way you would pitch an investor: problem, test results, numbers, ask. Every use case goes into a catalog the sponsor can act on, with a value-versus-complexity read. Teams leave able to explain and defend their case.

## 3. Sustainment (the part most programs skip)

- **Use-case tracker:** every use case, its owner, its stage (idea, shaped, funded, built, piloting, live, retired), and its value-versus-complexity quadrant. Review it with the sponsor monthly.
- **Office hours** weekly or biweekly to keep teams unblocked.
- **Change champions** recruited from each cohort to carry the habits back to their teams and report on how it is landing.
- **Before-and-after measurement:** repeat the discovery survey at 60 or 90 days, and report scoreboard results for the helpers that launched.
- **Prioritization sessions** to help the sponsor decide which cataloged use cases to fund next.

## What the team walks away with

- A prioritized catalog of use cases, each with a value-versus-complexity read
- Working helpers or validated solution shapes, not only ideas
- Business cases the team built and can defend
- Before-and-after measures of the program's impact
- A tracker and an office-hours rhythm that keep use cases moving

## Facilitator tips

- Use the team's real work from minute one. Generic examples teach the tool; their own examples teach the method.
- Show failures, uncut. People who are nervous about AI relax when they see that a failure is normal and fixable.
- Celebrate "not AI" verdicts as loudly as AI ones. The point is fixing the work.
- Keep the vocabulary light. Participants should leave with habits, not acronyms.
